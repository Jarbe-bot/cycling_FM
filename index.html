Tactics & Rules

1. The Engine (5 Phases)

  • Phase 1 (Possession): Midfielders dictate the play. Based heavily on Hills, Climbing and Physicality (GC).
  • Phase 2 (Chance Creation): Wingers and Attacking Mids break the lines using Hills and Sprint. Strikers act as decoys using GC or Sprint.
  • Phase 3 (Finishing): Strikers pull the trigger! Based entirely on Classics + Sprint (Fast) or Classics + GC (Target Man).
  • Phase 4 (Prevention): Fullbacks and CDM's cut the passing lanes early using Climbing and Sprint.
  • Phase 5 (The Block): The final wall. Centerbacks and Goalkeepers block the shots with brute TT and GC.

2. Rider Attributes

SPR (Sprint): Top speed in the final metres. On the pitch: pace, agility, wing play.
CLS (Classics): Power and killer instinct of a one-day racer. On the pitch: finishing, shot power.
TT (Time trial): Steady, unbreakable power against the clock. On the pitch: tackling, marking, shot stopping.
HIL (Hills): Punch and timing on short, steep climbs. On the pitch: technique, vision, passing.
CLB (Climbing): Patience and rhythm on long mountains. On the pitch: tactical awareness, interceptions.
GC (General classification): Stamina and consistency over three weeks. On the pitch: physicality, target man, shielding.

Base stats count fully up to 130. Above that every point still helps, at 40% strength.

3. Robin Hood Chemistry

Chemistry boosts apply to the specific player with the links! Link adjacent riders from the same Team (+1) and/or the same Nationality (+1). Legends form their own team. Green lines = Massive stat boost!

The Robin Hood Rule: Weak players receive a MUCH larger boost from chemistry than superstars like Pogačar. Building a tactical team is better than drafting loose stars!

4. The 100% Tactical Pie

No more "weak link" penalties! Your team's score per phase is a clean weighted average based on the positions you chose. A speedy Winger perfectly contributes to the Attack score, but a Defender does not drag it down. Put the right players in the right positions!

5. Off-Season: Action Points

  • Action Points: 3 per off-season, plus the bonus from the board objective you choose.
  • Transfers (1 AP): Swap out a weak link for a new scout. You must pick one of the four options.
  • Training (1 AP): Train one stat of one player. A training closes about a third of the gap to 130 (with a small dice roll): weak stats grow fast, elite stats barely. A second training on the same player in one off-season gives half.
  • Training Camp (2 AP): Your whole squad trains one stat. Every player improves a little, weak stats the most.
  • Position Swap (1 AP): Two players in the same line (attack, midfield or defence) trade places. The goalkeeper stays in goal.

6. The Board

  • What the board expects: Every season the board hopes for a finish one place better than your last two seasons. A reigning champion is expected to win again only if it is also the strongest club; otherwise the board asks it to fight for the title, without making the title itself a must. Everyone else is asked to qualify for the Continental Cup at most. Every cup round you win pleases the board too. In your first season the board judges your squad: a favourite is asked to fight for the title, a strong squad for a top 3 to 5 finish, and anyone else to stay clear of the bottom places.
  • The board's mood: From Ecstatic to Furious. The board evaluates whether you improve the squad (the wrong stats on the wrong players do not count) and how you finish compared to what it hoped for. A bad season hurts more than a good one helps, and repeated bad seasons hurt more each time. Tap the Board badge to see how it feels.
  • Furious means last chance. A board that has lost all faith sacks you, and so does finishing last from season 2 onwards.
  • Title odds: Tap ODDS to see the title race, with every club's chance of winning the league.

7. League & Continental Cup

  • Formations: every formation has its own style. 4-2-4 goes all-out attack (most goals, weak midfield, open games), 5-4-1 parks the bus (fewest goals conceded, lone striker), 3-5-2 controls midfield but is open on the flanks, 4-2-3-1 controls the game, 4-4-2 is compact and solid, 4-3-3 attacks wide, 3-4-3 presses high over the flanks but is thin at the back, the 4-1-2-1-2 diamond overloads the centre without width, 5-3-2 defends deep and counters with two strikers, and 4-4-1-1 runs everything through its second striker. Overall they are equally strong.
  • League: 8 clubs, 7 matchdays. The top 2 qualify for the Continental Cup. Rival clubs start strong and keep growing, fast at first and then slower and slower, so standing still means falling behind.5% stronger (up to +30%), so standing still means falling behind. The strong clubs stay strong and the weak stay weak.
  • Continental Cup: Four knockout rounds against Europe's elite, each round tougher than the last. Cup nights are open games with more chances, so the better team usually wins. Every round is tougher than the last, and the final is the toughest test of all: the finalist is one of Europe's best and arrives in unpredictable form. Cup opponents also grow every season.
